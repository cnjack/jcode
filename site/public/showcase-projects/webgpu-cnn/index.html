What a convolution learns

A 3×3 conv filter is a tiny pattern detector: it slides over the image and lights up wherever that pattern appears. The eight first-layer filters below (in the Visualizations panel) each learn a different edge or blob. Blue weights suppress ink, orange weights excite it; after ReLU only the positive responses survive, which is what the activation maps show.

Why WebGPU makes this fast

Every output pixel of a convolution is independent, so a GPU can compute thousands of them in parallel threads; that is what the WebGPU compute shaders here do. This model is tiny and also trains fine on CPU, but the same kernels scale to much larger nets where the per-thread parallelism is the whole point. The badge in the header reports which backend is actually running.

Reading the activation maps

Each tile is one filter's response to whatever you drew. Bright pixels mean that filter's pattern fired strongly there. As training progresses, some filters specialize — a vertical-edge detector lights up on the stem of a 1, a curve detector on the bowl of a 0. Maps that stay dark mean that filter rarely activates for this kind of input.
